## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NAVAS must now be treated as an ecosystem, not a single application. The legacy vision correctly identified the need for AI, mobile-money billing, multi-channel alerts, hardware agnosticism, low-bandwidth operation, and industry-specific expansion. However, it remained too broad, too monolithic, and too weak on operating detail. The new mandate is therefore to replace ambition without control with ambition plus executable structure. The v26.03.15 rewrite establishes the following strategic position: 1. NAVAS is a five-part ecosystem. a. UKO Core Basic is the lean, bandwidth-conscious operational monitoring base.

<!-- page 3 -->
b. Add-On Apps Library contains upgrade modules that increase wallet value and solve deeper operational problems. c. Marketplace exposes strategic enterprise connectors, OEM integrations, and third-party monetization. d. CMS is the management nervous system for governance, billing, service delivery, incidents, analytics, and control. e. Odoo-hosted eShop is the commercial storefront and transactional backbone for website discovery, quotation flow, order conversion, invoicing, payments, subscriptions, inventory, and revenue recognition. 2. PPMM becomes the commercial grammar of the ecosystem. NAVAS will no longer sell randomly named technical features. It will sell named products, grouped into service types, bound to billing plans, expressed consistently in Odoo, PFIs, quotes, invoices, websites, dashboards, and customer support language. 3. Token billing becomes the economic engine. Tokens are not a side feature and not a gimmick. Tokens become the atomic billing language capable of pricing time, events, parameters, AI inferences, bandwidth, storage, compliance value, marketplace visibility, and commercial outcomes. Billing must answer YES to any valid future pricing question. 4. Customer service becomes part of product design. The platform must actively reduce customer effort, reduce MTTR, improve CSAT, make renewals easier, trigger repeat sales opportunities, and protect field margins. The system must surface the next best service action for staff and the next best buy for customers. 5. Odoo is the transactional source of truth; NAVAS is the operational and monetization engine. Odoo owns catalog, CRM, quotations, subscriptions, invoices, payments, inventory, and many workflow approvals. NAVAS owns telemetry, rules, entitlements, AI, token metering, customer-facing monitoring, and operational intelligence. Both systems must be tightly integrated but not confused. 6. Architecture must remain digital-first and physical-last. Hardware remains necessary but must not dominate thinking. High-margin value will come from intelligence, compliance, workflow automation, analytics, premium support, platform integrations, recurring apps, and measurable reduction of waste. 7. Field service must become location-aware, budget-aware, and rotation-aware. Regional technicians must be deployed for speed and cost discipline. Local-first hiring reduces travel waste, while structured rotation prevents complacency. Site budgets, motorcycle economics, preventive maintenance cadence, and escalation discipline must all support MTTR targets. 8. The CMS must be built for management at scale.

## LEGACY VISION AUDIT AND REWRITE DELTA

The legacy vision document served an important purpose: it captured the transition from NARVA into NAVAS and correctly identified East African telematics opportunities such as AI, flexible billing, mobile money, low-bandwidth service, and expansion into people, goods, and wider IoT use cases. It also benchmarked global leaders and documented broad roles, high-level tech-stack logic, and early monetization thought. That document is now insufficient for the following reasons: 1. It mixed strategy, aspiration, backlog ideas, and product categories without enough separation. 2. It did not fully distinguish UKO Core Basic from Apps, Marketplace, eShop, and CMS. 3. It did not enforce the 3D commercial language across website, CRM, PFIs, invoices, CMS, and product UI. 4. It treated billing as flexible, but not yet as a formal pricing engine. 5. It did not sufficiently operationalize customer service, collections, retention, repeat revenue, and field budget control. 6. It borrowed Wialon hierarchy ideas, but did not convert them into a robust cross-border governance architecture for NAVAS. 7. It named many opportunities, but did not sufficiently define the engineering consequences of each opportunity. 8. It did not fully resolve the difference between customer-friendly naming and internal inventory/engineering naming. 9. It left too much room for feature creep in the CMS and the core. 10. It did not fully anchor Odoo as the website, commerce, and transactional backbone. This rewrite therefore introduces ten major corrections: A. Commercial correction Every product, service type, billing plan, and upsell path is aligned to PPMM and Odoo nomenclature.

## DESIGN PRINCIPLES AND NON-NEGOTIABLE ARCHITECTURE RULES

Principle 1: PPMM is the single commercial grammar. Every customer-facing label in NAVAS, CMS, Odoo, eShop, quotation, invoice, report, WhatsApp message, training manual, and dashboard must map back to an approved PPMM product/service type. Principle 2: One commercial product can have many technical components. Customers buy OLIWA, MAFUTA FLS, MDVR+AI, LOGISTICS, VEBA, or HELP DESK & TRAINING. They do not buy an accidental collection of raw IO names, obscure SKUs, or inconsistent engineering phrases.

<!-- page 8 -->
Principle 3: Customer-facing naming must remain human-readable. Internal references, stock codes, serial logic, and technical specs must live in internal reference, variant, serial, and technical fields—not as the public product name. Principle 4: UKO Core Basic must stay lean. If a feature is not necessary for base monitoring, identity, health, rules, reporting, or billing entitlement, it does not belong in UKO Core Basic. Principle 5: Add-On Apps must remain modular. Apps must be provisionable, removable, trialable, tokenized, and in some cases purchasable without forcing UKO Core Basic. Principle 6: Marketplace is for lock-in via integrations, not bloat. Marketplace connectors must deepen customer dependency by linking NAVAS to ERP, fuel cards, TMS, tax systems, OEM systems, and transactional flows. Principle 7: Odoo owns commerce; NAVAS owns telemetry and operational intelligence. No blurred ownership. This rule prevents accounting confusion, stock mismatches, and commercial leakage. Principle 8: Every billable act must be explainable. No "mystery charges." Every token charge must reconcile to a usage event, rule, or entitlement. Principle 9: Customer service is a design layer. Training, onboarding, self-service, incident response, prevention, communications, and renewal support must be designed into workflows. Principle 10: Automation is allowed only where logic is stable and auditable. Where decisions affect margin, legal exposure, strategic customers, or client trust, maker-checker and HITL/HIC remain mandatory. Principle 11: Local-first field support reduces structural waste. NAVAS must be built to support regional technicians, local stocking, route-efficient dispatch, and remote support before truck-rolls. Principle 12: AI must earn the right to exist. If AI does not reduce customer effort, improve insight, protect margin, or grow revenue, it will not be prioritized. Principle 13: The architecture must support both Wialon-style governance and NAVAS-specific monetization. Borrow best practices. Do not copy limitations.

<!-- page 9 -->
Principle 14: The system must remain future-proof against product expansion. New product lines, new sensors, new events, new markets, and new billing concepts must not require architecture rework. Principle 15: Documentation is part of delivery. A feature is incomplete if sales, operations, support, training, QMS, and finance cannot describe and support it consistently.
